## Chapter 83 — The parity side-quest: 75% of calculator.net by feature, 100% by correctness — and the gaps cluster into two shapes (2026-06-14)

The operator opened a side-quest: *"ask the PM agent to cross check between all completed calculator against the source on calculator.net to determine to what quantitative extent we are matching what's there to what we have."* The assistant routed it to the PM (it has WebFetch and owns docs/status synthesis) and dogfooded the freshly-merged `bin/serve-headless` to stand the app up for the audit — *"#116's script is validated in practice too,"* working first try.

The PM's audit produced a quantitative answer with two findings the assistant surfaced as the ones that matter. First, the headline: **~75% feature-weighted parity (71/95 source features), 80% simple mean — and 100% correctness.** Per-calculator parity ranged from 100% (Percentage, Tip, MMR — the last *exceeding* its source with four extra stats) down to 53% (BMI, missing child/teen mode, Age/Gender, healthy-weight/BMI-Prime/Ponderal). Second, and more strategically useful: *"**Every gap is *scope*, never *accuracy*** — 100% correctness parity: every calculator reproduces its source to the figure across its reference table,"* and *"**The 24 gaps collapse into two reusable *shapes*** — **inverse / multi-mode solving** and **input-side unit conversion** — not 24 one-offs."* The assistant drew the leverage point: encoding those two patterns into the agents once would lift parity across the *whole* 191-calculator backlog — a stronger candidate iteration theme than chasing per-calculator long-tail outputs.
